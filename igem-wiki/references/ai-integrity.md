# AI use, scientific integrity, and attribution

Use this reference whenever AI materially assists research, writing, coding, modeling, figures, translation, stakeholder analysis, or wiki implementation. The live iGEM AI, attribution, safety, privacy, intellectual-property, and communication policies take precedence.

Official policy entry point: <https://competition.igem.org/about/rules-and-policies>

## Non-negotiable boundaries

- Human team members remain responsible for understanding, checking, and defending all submitted work.
- AI output is not experimental evidence, a stakeholder statement, a source, or validation.
- Never invent or infer missing experiments, raw data, citations, interviews, consent, iteration history, Registry work, or attribution.
- Verify scientific claims against primary literature and team artifacts. Verify citations by opening the cited source.
- Do not upload confidential, personal, identifiable, unpublished, security-sensitive, or restricted biological information without authorization and an appropriate data-handling basis.
- Preserve authorship boundaries. Record outside help, reused work, and AI assistance where current iGEM policy or scientific integrity requires it.

## AI assistance register

For each material use, record:

| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| Tool and version | Product/model when known |
| Date and team owner | Who initiated and checked the work |
| Task | Research, drafting, code, figure, translation, analysis, or other |
| Inputs | Team artifacts and sources supplied; exclude secrets and unnecessary personal data |
| Output used | What was retained, revised, or rejected |
| Verification | Data, code, source, expert, or manual checks performed |
| Disclosure location | Attributions Form, wiki note, repository, caption, methods, or not required after policy review |
| Residual limitation | What remains uncertain or machine-dependent |

## Domain checks

### Scientific writing and literature

- Open every cited source and confirm it supports the nearby claim.
- Distinguish paraphrase, quotation, team interpretation, and source conclusion.
- Reject fabricated identifiers, quotations, statistics, and bibliographic details.

### Code, models, and data analysis

- Require a human-readable method, source data path, environment, parameters, and a reproducible execution route.
- Review generated code, run representative cases, and retain failures that change interpretation.
- For learned models, document dataset provenance, license, split strategy, leakage checks, metrics, uncertainty, and applicability domain.

### Images and figures

- Do not present generated scientific imagery as microscopy, experimental observation, structural prediction, or measured data.
- Label conceptual or AI-generated illustrations appropriately and keep a record of source assets and edits.
- Generate plots from traceable data and code; verify axes, units, statistics, and captions independently.

### Human Practices and personal data

- Do not infer stakeholder views or manufacture quotations.
- Check consent, anonymity, storage, translation, and representativeness before processing interview or survey material.
- Keep the stakeholder's statement separate from the team's interpretation and decision.

## Before publication

1. Review the current official AI policy rather than relying on this snapshot.
2. Audit AI-assisted claims, citations, code, figures, and translations.
3. Confirm that team members can explain and reproduce the submitted work.
4. Add appropriate disclosure and attribution without overstating the AI system's authorship or authority.
5. Remove private inputs, hidden prompts, credentials, and local machine paths from public artifacts.
